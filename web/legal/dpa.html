1. Scope and roles

This Data Processing Addendum ("DPA") governs the processing of personal data by [[COMPANY_NAME]] on behalf of a customer of the hosted DataAgent service. It applies where [[COMPANY_NAME]] acts as a processor and the customer acts as the controller of that personal data. Where [[COMPANY_NAME]] acts as a controller in its own right (for example, for billing administration or security logging that is not attributable to the customer), the Privacy Policy applies instead.

This DPA takes precedence over the Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy on the subject of processing personal data on the controller's behalf.

2. Definitions

  • Personal data, personal data breach, controller, processor, data subject, supervisory authority and processing have the meanings given in the UK GDPR and the EU GDPR (together, "GDPR").
  • Applicable Data Protection Law means the GDPR, the UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018, and any other law to which a party is subject that relates to the protection of personal data.
  • Service means the hosted DataAgent service described in the Terms of Service.

4. Processing instructions

The processor processes personal data only on the controller's documented instructions, including with regard to international transfers, unless required to do so by law to which the processor is subject. If the law requires it, the processor informs the controller before processing, unless the law prohibits that notice on important grounds of public interest.

The controller's instructions include the configuration of the Service: which identity provider is used, which model provider serves the assistant, which sub-processors are enabled, the retention window, and the roles and permissions assigned to each user.

The processor may not sell personal data, may not process it for its own purposes that are incompatible with the controller's instructions, and may not combine the controller's personal data with data from another customer except where the controller has instructed it to.

5. Security measures

The processor implements the following technical and organisational measures:

  • Tenant isolation. Each controller organisation is assigned a dedicated tenant. Its tables live in a dedicated DuckDB schema and its raw uploads in a dedicated directory, so one customer's data is not reachable from another's query path.
  • Encryption. TLS for data in transit; encryption at rest provided by the hosting and storage sub-processors. Application keys (the session-signing key and the entitlement-signing key) are held separately from the data and are excluded from routine backups.
  • Access control. Role-based access control with a deny-by-default permission model for mutating operations; named, least-privilege administrator accounts; multi-factor authentication for privileged access.
  • Authentication. Passwords are stored as salted bcrypt hashes. Sessions are signed, expire, and can be revoked.
  • Input and query validation. Identifiers taken from a request are validated against a strict pattern before they reach SQL; the SQL surface refuses references to another tenant's schema and to the system catalogues; the Python sandbox blocks filesystem access by capability.
  • Upload validation. Uploads are checked by content sniffing rather than by file extension alone, and are size- and type-limited.
  • Audit trail. Security-relevant actions are recorded in a tamper-evident audit trail that can be shipped to a shared, durable sink.
  • Availability. Health probes for liveness, readiness and per-dependency status; documented backup and restore procedure with periodic restore drills.
  • Development. Peer review of changes touching authentication, authorisation, payments or SQL, plus automated regression tests for each security control.

7. International transfers

Personal data may be transferred outside the UK and the EEA to the regions listed above. Where a transfer is not covered by an adequacy decision, the processor relies on the UK International Data Transfer Addendum and the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, together with a documented transfer risk assessment that considers the laws and practices of the destination country and the technical measures in place.

On written request, the processor provides the information needed to demonstrate that the applicable transfer mechanism is in place, including a copy of the executed clauses.

8. Data subject rights

Where the processor receives a request from a data subject about personal data it processes on the controller's behalf, it forwards the request to the controller without undue delay and does not respond itself unless the controller has instructed it to do so. The processor assists the controller, by appropriate technical and organisational measures, to respond to requests under the GDPR: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection.

The processor makes available the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the accountability principle, and makes available the information the controller needs to carry out a data protection impact assessment.

9. Audit and inspection

The processor makes available all information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with the GDPR and this DPA, and allows for and contributes to audits, including inspections, conducted by the controller or a auditor the controller mandates, at most once a year unless a personal data breach or a supervisory authority requires more frequent audits.

Where an audit requires access to the production environment, the parties agree the scope, timing and safeguards in advance, and the audit does not unreasonably interfere with the operation of the Service or with other customers' data.

10. Security incidents

The processor notifies the controller without undue delay, and in any event within 48 hours, after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting the controller's personal data. The notification describes the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records affected, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.

The processor documents every personal data breach, including the facts, its effects and the remedial action taken. Where notification to a supervisory authority is required, the processor provides the controller with the information needed for that notification. Communications to data subjects are coordinated with the controller unless the processor is required by law to communicate directly.

11. Return and deletion

On termination of the Service, the processor returns or deletes the controller's personal data, at the controller's election, subject to the retention window in the Privacy Policy. The processor deletes the tenant schema, the raw uploads and the platform rows belonging to the controller's organisation, and ages the affected backups out on the rolling backup schedule.

The processor keeps personal data in encrypted form for the retention window only for the purposes of restoration and legal compliance, and does not use it for any other purpose in the meantime.
